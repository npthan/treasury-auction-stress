# Project Plan

## Research question

Using only information publicly available **before** a U.S. Treasury
auction, can we forecast the distribution of primary-dealer take-down
and identify auctions likely to require unusually heavy dealer
balance-sheet absorption?

This is a research project about auction mechanics and dealer
balance-sheet stress, **not** a Treasury-direction trading strategy. No
Sharpe ratio or trading P&L is a project goal. Success is measured by
whether the forecasts are honest, well-calibrated, and free of data
leakage — not by whether they would have made money.

## Initial research universe

Nominal coupon Treasury auctions:

- 2-year, 3-year, 5-year, 7-year, 10-year, 20-year, 30-year notes/bonds
- Coverage target: approximately 2010 onward, subject to actual data
  availability and schema consistency in the official source
- New issues and reopenings are treated as **separate** observations,
  not merged, because dealers and the market price them differently
  (a reopening adds to an existing, already-trading CUSIP with known
  liquidity characteristics; a new issue starts a fresh trading line)

Bills, TIPS, and floating-rate notes are explicitly **out of scope**
for the initial phases and are documented as future extensions only.
They must not delay the nominal-coupon work.

## Modeling sequence

Baselines are deliberately simple before anything complex, because the
number of nominal coupon auctions per year is small (roughly 60-90
across all seven tenors) and Treasury auction dynamics are already
fairly well explained by a handful of structural variables (tenor,
reopening status, offering size, and slowly-moving regime):

1. Tenor-specific historical mean or median -- **implemented, Phase 6** (`baseline_tenor_mean`)
2. Tenor-and-reopening historical baseline -- **implemented, Phase 6** (`baseline_tenor_reopening_mean`, the pre-declared strong baseline)
3. Rolling historical baseline -- **implemented, Phase 6** (`baseline_recent_history`; plus `baseline_global_mean`, added as a fourth, even simpler baseline)
4. Regularized linear regression -- **implemented, Phase 6** (Ridge, structural-only and core predictor tiers)
5. Elastic net -- **implemented, Phase 6** (core predictor tier)
6. Gradient-boosted trees -- **implemented, Phase 7** (`challenger_gbm_core`, `sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingRegressor`; did not beat either recent-history baseline in the backtest)
7. Quantile regression -- **implemented, Phase 7** (`quantile_recent_history_residual`, walk-forward residual quantiles around the frozen point forecast; `quantile_gbm_core`, one `HistGradientBoostingRegressor(loss="quantile")` fit per quantile level)
8. A hierarchical model that partially pools information across tenors -- **implemented, Phase 7**, as an empirical-Bayes (James-Stein-style) shrinkage estimator, not a full Bayesian hierarchical model (`challenger_shrinkage_tenor_reopening`; did not beat either recent-history baseline)
9. A regime-aware model -- **implemented, Phase 7**: `baseline_recent_history_adaptive` updates within the test year using a safe, leakage-audited regime feature (see `artifacts/phase_6_evaluation_protocol.md`'s Dealer Absorption Surprise treatment for why the original Phase 6 regime feature was deferred, and the Phase 7 acceptance review for the safe replacement); it was the empirically strongest model in the entire project
10. Rolling or adaptive uncertainty calibration -- **implemented, Phase 7** (walk-forward residual-quantile calibration; pooled 90%-interval coverage was near nominal but drifted materially by year -- see `artifacts/phase_7_probabilistic_results.md` and `artifacts/phase_8_interpretation.md`)

Neural networks are deliberately excluded as a starting point given the
small sample size.

## Evaluation philosophy

Phase 6 implements **chronological rolling-origin** evaluation exactly
as planned here -- auctions are never shuffled, because that would let
a model trained on the future "predict" the past, which is not a
real-world forecasting scenario. Phase 6's implemented metrics: MAE,
median absolute error, RMSE, signed bias, and out-of-sample R² against
a pre-declared strong baseline (`artifacts/phase_6_baseline_results.md`),
broken down by test year, tenor, and reopening status, with the
provisional partial year always reported separately. Pinball loss, prediction-interval coverage/width, PR-AUC and Brier
score for stress events, and reliability diagrams are all implemented
in **Phase 7** (`artifacts/phase_7_probabilistic_results.md`,
`artifacts/phase_7_stress_event_gate.md`) -- Phase 6 itself produced only
point forecasts, not probabilistic ones. Feature ablations remain
out of scope for both phases and were deliberately not run in Phase 8
either, to avoid any outer-test-score-selected feature search; see
`artifacts/phase_8_interpretation.md`'s feature-importance section for
the reasoning.
