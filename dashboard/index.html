Headline finding

In this retrospective backtest (11 complete test years, 2015–2025, plus a provisional partial year 2026), a simple recency-based baseline that is allowed to update within the test year (adaptive) beat the same baseline frozen for the whole year (frozen), and both beat two more complex challengers (a gradient-boosted-tree model and a shrinkage estimator). Recency and safely-available within-year updates mattered more than model complexity in this sample. The two baselines use different information schedules (see the chart note below) — this is not a claim that "adaptive" is a fundamentally better model family, only that it had a real, disclosed information advantage.

⚠ Proxy, not stress. Dealer take-down share is an auction-allocation measure — at most a proxy for balance-sheet absorption, not a direct measure of dealers' final holdings, funding stress, or auction "success."
⚠ Stress classifier: gate passed, calibration failed. Enough stress events existed to evaluate the classifier, but its probabilities are roughly 2–2.3× worse (Brier score) than a trivial no-skill forecast. It is not a reliable stress detector.
⚠ Coverage drifts by year. The residual-quantile method's ~92% pooled 90%-interval coverage hides real under-coverage early and over-coverage late in the sample — pooled coverage alone is not evidence of stable calibration.

One cutoff and one tenor apply to every chart on this page at once — charts never mix an announcement-cutoff number with a pre-auction one, or blend the provisional 2026 partial year into a complete-year average. The one exception is the "MAE by tenor" chart just below: since its whole purpose is comparing all seven tenors side by side, selecting a tenor there highlights that tenor's bars among all of them rather than hiding the other six — every other panel on this page (KPIs, MAE by year, coverage/width, the stress panel, and case studies) is recomputed on ONLY the selected tenor's own auctions. Each panel's own sample size (n) is shown below it so a small-sample tenor is never mistaken for a pooled one.

Stress-event classifier vs. no-skill baseline

⚠ The classifier's Brier score is worse than always predicting each fold's own training-event rate ("no-skill"). Its probabilities should not be trusted as a stress indicator, despite non-trivial ranking ability (PR-AUC).

"Gate passed" means there were enough labeled stress events (≥30 pooled, ≥5 per shown year) to evaluate the classifier at all — it is not a claim of good performance. When a single tenor is selected below, this panel recomputes on only that tenor's own labeled auctions; several tenors fall under the 5-positive gate used elsewhere in this project once split out this way, and the panel says so explicitly rather than showing an unreliable number silently.

What this dashboard does not show

  • Dealer balance-sheet stress. primary_dealer_share is an allocation share at the moment of auction settlement, not a measure of dealer capital, funding cost, inventory risk, or distress. High take-down can reflect normal intermediation timing, not market stress.
  • Causal inference. Every comparison here is a retrospective, observational backtest over years (2015–2025, provisional 2026) already examined during earlier project phases — not a fresh holdout, and not evidence that any feature or model "causes" a given outcome.
  • Trading returns. No Sharpe ratio, P&L, or trading strategy is computed or implied anywhere in this project. Forecast accuracy is evaluated purely as a research question about auction mechanics.
  • A validated stress detector. See the calibration warning above — the classifier's probabilities are not reliable, regardless of its PR-AUC.
  • Live or future data. This dashboard reads a static, generated snapshot (dashboard/data/phase8_dashboard_data.json) of the accepted Phase 7 backtest. It does not call any external API and will not update itself.
